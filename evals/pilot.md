# Two-week pilot worksheet

Use pseudonymous participant IDs. Recruit at least five knowledge workers and five developers; obtain opt-in participation. Participants keep raw histories local to their chosen provider environment. This is a study plan, not an enrollment record.

## First session

Record: participant ID, audience, host/version, available history scope, whether setup succeeded, whether the user had to select conversations, time to first useful result, whether a discovery was worth attention, whether a delivered result would be adopted/tested, and any critical failure. Invite a short explanation rather than requiring a long survey.

Do not count a useful-looking report without a usable result as adoption. When checking source accuracy, the participant can inspect it privately and report pass/fail without sending the history.

## Days 1–14

No default reminders. Ask participants to note each self-initiated use: date, trigger (model/tool update, restart, changed evidence/constraints, stuck work, cross-chat synthesis, open discovery), usefulness, adopted/tested result, repetition, and whether it felt intrusive. Record non-use without pressuring daily engagement.

At the end ask: What made you remember this tool? Was there a moment it should have been available but wasn't? Did it surface anything you did not want revisited? Would you choose periodic discovery, and under what conditions?

## Summary

Report denominators, setup failures, first-session relevance/adoption counts, non-model-update repeat use, critical failures, and negative feedback separately by audience. With ten participants treat percentages as exploratory, not population estimates. The 70%/50% targets are hypotheses. Do not introduce background scheduling just to increase recorded usage.
